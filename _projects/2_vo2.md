For my bachelor's thesis, I studied how the metal-insulator transition (MIT) temperature in VO₂ microwires can be modulated via controlled nano-indentation (Park AFM NX20) and in-situ opto-thermal stimulation. I used Raman spectroscopy as both a probe and a stimulus across a range of laser power densities, times, and temperatures to track the transition, then used TEM to understand the geometry of my wires. COMSOL simulations helped connect the local strain/thermal fields to what I was seeing experimentally. I am currently extending this work using Scanning Precession Electron Diffraction (SPED) to provide additional orientation and phase-mapping insights.
